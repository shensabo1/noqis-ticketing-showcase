    Why the record can be trusted

    What happens when an agent saves a change

    Each save is one database transaction. If any step fails, nothing from the save is kept, including its audit entry.

    1

    Check who

    A signed-in agent whose session passed its authenticator code. Permissions come from one registry, and refusals are audited.

    2

    Check the rules

    Allowed status moves come from the database. Stale edits are refused.

    3

    Change the ticket

    The ticket row is updated and its version number goes up.

    4

    Add to the timeline

    A ticket_events row, marked internal or customer-visible. The database refuses a ticket change without one.

    5

    Write the audit entry

    Last write before commit. Locks the chain, links to the previous entry.

    6

    Commit

    All of it is saved together, or none of it is.

    Steps 2 to 5 run inside one transaction. The app logs in as the restricted noqis_app role and refuses to start with owner rights

    The audit chain

    Each entry stores a fingerprint (SHA-256 hash) of itself plus the previous entry's fingerprint. Editing or deleting an old entry breaks every link after it, and the verifier reports where.

    seq 61 · NOQ-1002priority.changedprev efe9…hash d1e4…
    seq 62 · NOQ-1002assignment.changedprev d1e4…hash a513…
    seq 63 · NOQ-1004status.changedprev a513…hash c33c…
    seq 64 · NOQ-1004note.addedprev c33c…hash afe7…

    The four most recent entries in the local database, with hashes shortened to their first four characters. Each “prev” matches the “hash” before it. pnpm db:verify-audit checks the whole chain, and CI runs it on every pull request.

    Promises in the code

    Rules the system enforces

    Audit data can't be changed

    Database triggers reject UPDATE, DELETE, and TRUNCATE on the audit log and the timeline, even for the database owner and on each monthly partition.

    0002_audit_log.sql · 0005_tickets.sql

    Internal notes stay internal

    Notes are marked internal on the timeline and look different in the console. An agent's reason for a status change is internal too. Audit entries store message IDs, never message text.

    tickets.test.ts

    Closed means closed

    A closed ticket can't be edited or receive messages. The API refuses, and so does a database trigger.

    reject_closed_ticket_update()

    Options are data

    Statuses, allowed moves, priorities, and categories load from config/seed/. Re-seeding never overwrites an admin's edit.

    db/seed.ts

    Someone owns active work

    A ticket can't leave New without an assignee. The first public reply assigns the ticket to whoever sent it.

    TICKET_LIFECYCLE.md "Assignment"

    The dev shortcut can't ship

    The agent switcher exists only when NODE_ENV=development, and only on this machine's own address. In production the route returns 404 and the header is ignored.

    api.test.ts · foundation.test.ts

    The app can't use owner rights

    The server logs in as a restricted role that can't disable the audit triggers, and refuses to start if it is given the database owner's login.

    assertApplicationRole() · api.test.ts

    Customers stay separate

    Every customer read is scoped to the caller's company, and Postgres row-level security repeats the rule. A cross-tenant test suite proves it, and anything a customer can't see answers "not found".

    tenancy-*.test.ts

    Notes never reach customers

    The customer view is built from an allowlist. A test plants secret markers in every internal place and proves none of them appear, including on merged and follow-up tickets.

    tenancy-note-leak.test.ts

    Every change has its record

    A ticket change can't commit without its timeline entry from the same transaction, and the database checks it at commit time.

    0022 · ticket-timeline.test.ts

    Humans answer customers

    AI never writes or sends replies. The only automatic email is a plainly labeled receipt; any AI help is for agents only and is never sent.

    CLAUDE.md rule 1

    Logs help, without leaking

    Logs keep user names, emails, IP addresses, and correlation IDs for troubleshooting. Passwords, tokens, and customer message text are removed.

    logger.ts · foundation.test.ts

    Settled so far

    Decisions made

    These start as sensible defaults and the team's own answers, and can be changed later.

    Customer updates

    P1: every 10 minutes, or kept updated by phone. P2: every 2 hours. P3: every 4 hours. P4: every 8 hours, in business hours. The site's first-response promises stand.

    D-04

    P1 at night

    PagerDuty pages the on-call rotation. Agents set P1, and a "site down" email from a Professional customer pages someone to check.

    D-05

    Receipts

    One plainly labeled automated receipt with a NOQ-1234 reference. Every real reply still comes from a named person.

    D-06

    Three queues

    Edge Ninjas for support, Billing for the billing department, and Escalations for tickets that need senior attention. Invoices stay in the CRM.

    D-08 · A-17

    Compliance

    Strong controls now, a legal review before launch, and no card data stored. Customers are warned not to send card numbers, health data, or keys.

    D-07

    Customer portal

    Customers sign in from the website's support login and see open and past tickets: status, priority (Low to Critical), whether it is pending an agent or customer update, and the conversation. They can open tickets and reply.

    D-11 · A-18

    Two update boxes

    On each ticket, agents get a Customer update box that goes to the customer, and an Internal update box only staff see. Every update is saved on the ticket. An @mention pings that teammate by email and Slack.

    A-19

    Unknown senders

    Email from an unrecognized address goes to a triage queue for a person to sort, with no automatic reply.

    D-09

    Branding and hosting

    NoQis branding only on customer email. Data stays in a US region, encrypted. The database is Supabase; the application host is still being chosen.

    D-10 · D-02

    Your Edge Ninja

    An opened ticket tells the customer "Your Edge Ninja is Maya", first name only, or "billing specialist" for billing. Staff see the whole team's workload by person.

    A-39

    People on a ticket

    The requester or a customer admin adds colleagues, who join at once from the company's own domain; other addresses wait for an agent's approval. Staff follow tickets with a Watch button.

    D-16 · A-29

    Replies and closing

    A customer reply moves a waiting or resolved ticket back to open; a reply after closing opens a linked follow-up. Customers can close their own tickets, with an optional note.

    A-40 · A-41

    Mail from someone new

    Email from someone not on a ticket is never held or attached: it opens a new ticket, linked to the one it mentioned so the agent sees the context.

    D-17

    AI for agents only

    An assistant may suggest troubleshooting steps and explain issues to newer agents, in a panel marked as AI. It never writes to customers.
